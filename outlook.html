The 287/114/35W corridor to 2035: what waiting will cost

The towns this corridor serves are among the fastest-growing in the country. Northlake has nearly tripled since the 2020 Census and Haslet has more than doubled. Fort Worth added more residents in 2025 than any U.S. city but one. Traffic on US 287, SH 114 and I-35W has grown 5.8% to 6.8% a year since 2016. The main-lane widening that would add capacity to US 287 ($589.6 million) is not in TxDOT's 10-year plan, with estimated letting in 2035.

This page projects what the corridor will cost travelers, businesses and the public between 2026 and 2035 if capacity is not added. It builds on the measured costs in the US 287 cost of delay and corridor pages and draws two conclusions: a conservative one that counts only out-of-pocket costs and assumes growth slows, and a realistic one that uses FHWA's standard crash valuation and assumes recent trends continue.

Independent analysis by a 76052 resident. Projections are the author's own estimates built on public data; they are not forecasts by TxDOT, TTI, NCTCOG or any agency. Not affiliated with TxDOT, TTI, FHWA, NCTCOG, the City of Fort Worth, the City of Haslet, the City of Rhome, or Tarrant, Denton or Wise County.

Growth is not slowing down

NCTCOG's January 2025 estimates show the North Texas region added 879,669 residents in five years, about 176,000 a year. The towns along this corridor grew many times faster. Census Bureau estimates released in May 2026 show Fort Worth added 19,512 residents between July 2024 and July 2025, second only to Charlotte among U.S. cities, and passed 1 million residents.

The starting point: what the corridor costs today

Two measured costs form the baseline. Congestion is TTI's measured cost of passenger and truck delay plus wasted fuel for 2024, on the five TTI segments that make up the corridor's highways. Crashes are every reported crash on the same highways, valued with FHWA's 2024 cost per crash by severity and averaged over 2023 to 2025.

Two conclusions for 2026 to 2035

Conservative
$1.15B

in congestion and out-of-pocket crash costs from 2026 through 2035, rising from $106M in 2026 to $124M in 2035. Add $180M in construction inflation on the widening: $1.33B.

  • Traffic and congestion cost grow 3% a year, about half the corridor's 2016–2025 traffic growth.
  • Crash costs held flat at the 2023–25 level.
  • Crashes counted at economic cost only (no quality-of-life losses).
  • Construction costs rise 3% a year.
  • 2024 dollars; no general inflation added.

The price of the fix is rising too

TxDOT's Highway Cost Index tracks what Texas pays for highway construction. Its 12-month average rose 74% from December 2019 to December 2024, then eased in 2025–26 but remains 44% above 2019. Every year the widening waits, it is likely to cost more. Meanwhile TxDOT's 2027 Unified Transportation Program, approved August 27, 2026, totals about $138 billion, down from about $146 billion in the 2026 program, and the US 287 main lanes are still not in it.

What this means

Even the floor is large

The conservative case counts only cash costs, assumes traffic growth halves and crashes stop rising. It still reaches $1.33B by 2035, about $291,000 a day in 2026 and $341,000 a day by 2035.

The realistic case is still restrained

It grows congestion cost more slowly than traffic, although delay normally rises faster than volume once a road passes capacity. It reaches about $1.4 million a day by 2035, $4.25B over ten years.

Not counted here

  • Development, jobs and tax base that locate elsewhere because the corridor is unreliable.
  • The extra congestion on I-35W south of the US 287 merge ($36.7M in 2024).
  • Delay on local roads as traffic diverts off the highways.
  • Emissions and vehicle wear beyond TTI's fuel estimate.

Neither conclusion is a forecast. Both show the same thing: on any reasonable assumption, the cost of leaving this corridor at its current capacity through 2035 is larger than the cost of the widening that is waiting for funding.

Method

Baseline. Congestion: TTI's 2024 annual congestion cost (passenger delay at $24.01 per person-hour, truck delay at $80.16 per truck-hour, plus wasted fuel) for five corridor segments. Crashes: TxDOT CRIS crashes on US 287 (I-35W to Rhome) and on SH 114 and I-35W inside the corridor, valued with FHWA 2024 KABCO costs per crash and averaged over 2023–2025.

Projection. Each year's cost = baseline × (1 + growth)year − 2024, summed for 2026–2035. All values in 2024 dollars. Construction inflation = $589.6M × ((1 + rate)9 − 1).

Growth rates. Conservative 3% (traffic and congestion) and 0% (crashes). Realistic 5.3% (TTI corridor congestion cost, 2019–2024) and 5% (crashes; corridor AADT grew 5.8–6.8% a year, 2016–2025). Construction: 3% and 4% (TxDOT HCI rose from 100 in 2012 to 175.9 in August 2026, about 4% a year).

Limitations

  • TTI congestion costs are in each report year's dollars; part of the 2019–2024 growth reflects updated values of time.
  • Fatal crashes are rare and drive comprehensive cost; single years swing widely.
  • V/C estimates are planning-level, not a traffic operations study.
  • Population figures are estimates; NCTCOG and Census methods differ.
  • The widening would not remove all of the projected cost; the US 287-only figures are the fairest comparison.
