The 287/114/35W Corridor: improved vs. unimproved segments

US 287, SH 114 and I-35W bound a study area of about 54 square miles covering Haslet, AllianceTexas and far north Fort Worth. The 76052 pages assess the arterial network inside a single ZIP code. This assessment covers the full corridor: all three boundary highways, their frontage roads, and every public road within the study area, using TxDOT crash records (CRIS), traffic counts (AADT) and the TxDOT Roadway Inventory.

The corridor offers a direct before-and-after comparison. One segment, I-35W from North Tarrant Parkway to Eagle Parkway, received a major capacity improvement (North Tarrant Express segment 3C, opened June 2023). There, the crash rate per vehicle-mile held steady as volumes grew. The remaining segments are still four-lane facilities: I-35W north of Eagle Parkway, SH 114 and US 287. On those segments, annual crashes rose by a factor of 1.8 to 4.4 between 2019 and 2025.

Independent analysis by a 76052 resident. Crash data comes from TxDOT's Crash Records Information System (CRIS), which compiles Texas Peace Officer's Crash Reports (CR-3).     Segment 1: I-35W, Eagle Parkway to SH 114

    North Tarrant Express segment 3C rebuilt I-35W from North Tarrant Parkway to Eagle Parkway, adding four managed lanes, auxiliary lanes and frontage roads. It opened in June 2023 and terminates at Eagle Parkway. For the remaining 2.5 miles to SH 114, 2026 aerial imagery shows two general-purpose lanes per direction with no managed lanes. This lane drop feeds directly into the I-35W / SH 114 interchange, the second-highest crash cluster in the study area.

    Segment 2: SH 114, US 287 to FM 156

    For about nine miles, SH 114 is a rural four-lane divided highway: two lanes per direction, a 70 mph posted speed, 17–18% truck share, and at-grade median crossovers and direct driveway access. Residential development now lines the north side. The first grade-separated interchange is at FM 156. AADT near FM 156 rose from about 23,000 in 2019 to 43,000 in 2025, a compound growth rate of about 11% a year.

    Segment 3: US 287, I-35W to SH 114 (four lanes, 14 miles)

    US 287 is a controlled-access freeway with one-way frontage roads, but its main lanes remain two per direction for the full 14 miles from I-35W to SH 114. At the south end, near North Tarrant Parkway and Harmon Road, AADT is about 81,000 with a 22% truck share. The north end, from Haslet to SH 114, carries lower volumes but has the fastest crash growth and the highest fatality count of any segment in the study area.

    Findings and limitations

    Findings

    • Study-area crashes rose from about 785 a year to about 1,260 in 2025, with 2026 pacing toward about 1,430.
    • On the improved I-35W segment, the crash rate per vehicle-mile held steady while volumes grew.
    • On the three unimproved four-lane segments, annual crashes rose by a factor of 1.6 to 4.2 between 2017–19 and 2025.
    • US 287 carries the highest fatality count of the three facilities. SH 114 crashes tripled as residential development filled in along a rural cross-section.
    • I-35W north of Eagle Parkway and the south end of US 287 are near peak-hour capacity now. At current growth, SH 114 reaches capacity by about 2030.

    Limitations

    • These are observed associations, not causal estimates. Population growth, driver familiarity, truck share, distraction and construction activity all contribute, and crash reports do not attribute outcomes to roadway capacity.
    • The improved I-35W segment is not a perfect control. Its interchange spacing, frontage roads and operating speeds differ from the other segments.
    • This assessment does not estimate how many of the 94 fatalities improvements would have prevented.

    Questions for TxDOT and regional partners

    NTE 3C demonstrates the effect of a capacity improvement on one leg of this corridor. The other legs carry comparable growth on cross-sections designed for rural volumes. Residents need committed schedules for I-35W from Eagle Parkway to SH 114, SH 114 west of FM 156, and US 287 from I-35W to SH 114.

    Methodology

    Crash records were retrieved from the TxDOT CRIS public query tool with a single map-area query for January 1, 2016 through September 27, 2026. The query polygon follows the three boundary highways and is buffered slightly so that crashes on both sides of each highway, including frontage roads, are captured. Crashes are assigned to a facility by the highway system and number on the report, or by street name where no highway is listed.

    Crash rates divide crashes by VMT: the mean AADT of TxDOT count stations on each facility × 365 × the facility's length along the study area. Lane configurations come from the TxDOT 2023 Roadway Inventory, verified against 2026 aerial imagery. Peak-direction V/C is AADT × K × D divided by 2,250 vehicles per lane per hour (2,200 on SH 114), adjusted for heavy-vehicle share.

    Data limitations

    • Because the query polygon is buffered, some far-side frontage-road crashes are included. This is intentional: they occur on the boundary facilities.
    • CRIS includes only reportable crashes documented on a CR-3 (generally an injury or at least $1,000 in property damage).
    • The 2023 TxDOT inventory predates the NTE 3C managed lanes. Aerial imagery was used for the improved segment.
    • 2030 projections extend the 2019–25 growth rate. Actual growth may differ.
    • US 287 figures cover the full 14 miles. The segment within ZIP 76052 has a higher rate; see the 76052 crash analysis.
