Where Fort Worth's road money goes

Fort Worth's far north and northwest (Council Districts 4, 7 and 10) hold 30% of the city's residents and absorbed 49% of its population growth between the 2010 and 2020 Censuses. They hold 8% of the TxDOT highway construction dollars that are funded or scheduled within ten years inside city limits. Most of the state money listed for these districts, 81%, sits in TxDOT's "Planning, 10+ years" category.

City bond money is a different story. Districts 4, 7 and 10 received 33% of the street and transportation bond dollars assigned to specific locations from 2014 to 2026, about in line with their share of residents. The gap is on the state highway side.

State highway projects (TxDOT)

TxDOT's Project Tracker lists 175 projects that run through Fort Worth city limits. Each project's estimated construction cost is split among council districts by the share of its length inside each one. "Near-term" covers projects under construction, starting soon, or starting within ten years. "Deferred" is TxDOT's "Planning, 10+ years" category.

The largest near-term state projects sit elsewhere: I-30 reconstruction in the west and central districts, I-820 and SH 183 in the east, and I-35W between downtown and Loop 820. In the far north, the main-lane widenings of US 287 and I-35W are listed for 10+ years. These freeways also carry regional traffic, so a dollar in one district serves drivers from many.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

The state gap is real

The fastest-growing part of the city has the smallest share of near-term state highway money for its size and growth. Its biggest state projects, the US 287 and I-35W widenings, are the ones listed for 10+ years.

The city is not the gap

City street bonds in Districts 4, 7 and 10 are roughly proportional to population. The city builds the roads it controls. US 287, SH 114 and I-35W are state roads.

Limits

  • TxDOT's tracker lists current and future projects, not completed ones.
  • Freeway dollars serve regional traffic, not only district residents.
  • Populations are 2020 Census; the north has grown faster since.
  • Haslet, Northlake and other towns on the corridor are outside city limits and not counted.

Method

Districts. City of Fort Worth council districts (current map), from the city's open data boundaries service.

TxDOT. All TxDOT Project Tracker projects intersecting the city (Fort Worth and Dallas districts), retrieved October 1, 2026. Each line was sampled every 100 m and its estimated construction cost split by length among districts; length outside city limits is excluded.

City bonds. City of Fort Worth Bond Tracker, Transportation department, approved funding for 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026 bonds, assigned by mapped location.

Population. 2010 and 2020 Census block groups (TIGERweb), assigned to districts by center point. The sum (899,219) is within 2% of the city's 2020 count.
